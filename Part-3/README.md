## Setup: proxy labels

No accident dataset is available, so Part 3 classification uses a **proxy**
accident-risk label. It demonstrates the classification workflow only and is
not a prediction of real accidents.

```powershell
python Part-3/prepare_labels.py
```

By default it reads `Part-2/data/processed/Metro_Interstate_Traffic_Volume_features.csv`
(run the Part 2 pipeline and feature engineering first), writes
`Part-3/data/processed/Metro_Interstate_Traffic_Volume_ml.csv`, and logs to
`Part-3/logs/prepare_labels.log`. Use `--input`, `--output`, `--log-file`, or
`--log-level` to override defaults.

The script adds these columns and keeps all Part 2 columns:

- `timestamp` – parsed `date_time`, for time-based splits.
- `weather_main` – restored from the one-hot `weather_main_*` columns.
- `is_low_visibility` – 1 when `weather_main` is Fog, Mist, Haze or Smoke.
- `congestion_category` – quartiles of `traffic_volume` (Low ≤ q1, Medium ≤ q2,
  High ≤ q3, Severe above). On this data q1/q2/q3 = 1,248.5 / 3,427 / 4,952,
  matching the known project quartiles, giving four near-equal classes.
- `high_risk` – 1 when congestion is High/Severe **and** (`weather_main` is in
  `SEVERE_WEATHER` = Thunderstorm, Snow, Squall **or** `is_low_visibility` = 1).
  This gives 2,964 of 40,575 hours (7.30%) positive, so use PR-AUC/F1 and class
  weights rather than accuracy.

The weather groupings are assumptions defined as constants in
[`prepare_labels.py`](prepare_labels.py); change them there if the definition
should differ.

**Shared features (28):** hour, day of week, month, weekend flag; cyclical
sin/cos of hour, day of week and month; a holiday flag spread over the whole
date; temperature (°C), rain, snow, cloud cover, rain/snow indicators; and
one-hot `weather_main`.

**Split:** time-based, first 80% of hours train (2012-10-02 to 2017-10-26 17:00,
32,460 rows), last 20% test (2017-10-26 18:00 to 2018-09-30, 8,115 rows). The
test period includes partially covered 2018. Fixed `random_state` of 42; the
classification threshold is fixed at 0.5.

**Models:** regression predicts `traffic_volume` (Linear Regression vs Random
Forest, plus reference baselines: train mean and mean by hour). Classification
predicts `high_risk` (Logistic Regression vs Random Forest, both with class
weights).

| Task | Model | Results on test set |
|---|---|---|
| Regression | Baseline: train mean | MAE 1,724; R² −0.000 |
| Regression | Baseline: mean by hour | MAE 622; R² 0.783 |
| Regression | Linear Regression | MAE 801; R² 0.735 |
| Regression | Random Forest | MAE 239; R² 0.959 |
| Classification | Logistic Regression | Acc 0.957; Prec 0.689; Rec 0.993; F1 0.814; ROC AUC 0.995 |
| Classification | Random Forest | Acc 0.986; Prec 0.880; Rec 0.986; F1 0.930; ROC AUC 0.997 |

Interpretation: Random Forest is best on both tasks. The linear regression
does not beat the mean-by-hour baseline because traffic is non-linear in hour
(bimodal), which supports the project finding that time of day dominates.
Random Forest regression importance is concentrated in the hour features.

**Classification scores are inflated by design.** `high_risk` is defined from
`traffic_volume` and `weather_main`, and the shared feature set includes the
`weather_main` one-hot columns (as requested). The models are largely
re-learning the label rule (high-volume hours combined with listed weather),
so the near-perfect ROC AUC does not show real accident-prediction skill. The
test positive rate (9.4%) also differs from train (6.8%), and the label
quartiles were computed on all rows (see Cautions). Results are associations,
single time split, no hyperparameter tuning or cross-validation.

## Task 2: unsupervised models

```powershell
python Part-3/unsupervised_models.py
```

It reads the labelled CSV, logs to `Part-3/logs/unsupervised_models.log`, writes
to `Part-3/outputs/` (`kmeans_k_selection.csv`, `kmeans_cluster_profile.csv`,
`kmeans_cluster_assignments.csv`, `association_rules.csv`,
`unsupervised_run_info.json`) and logs one MLflow run in the experiment
`task2-unsupervised-models`. Options: `--k`, `--min-support`,
`--min-confidence`, plus the same `--input`, `--output-dir`, `--log-file`,
`--log-level` and MLflow options as Task 1. No new dependency; the rule mining
uses pandas. Notebook:
[`notebooks/Task2_unsupervised_models.ipynb`](notebooks/Task2_unsupervised_models.ipynb).

No accident data exists, so both methods describe **traffic conditions**, not
accidents.

**K-means:** features are hour (sine/cosine), weekend flag, weather severity
(0 other, 1 low visibility = Fog/Mist/Haze/Smoke, 2 severe =
Thunderstorm/Snow/Squall, reusing the Part 3 groups) and `traffic_volume`, all
standardised. k = 2 to 10 were scored (elbow and silhouette); **k = 6 was chosen
for interpretability** (a judgement: silhouette keeps rising to k = 9, but is
flat from k = 5 to 6). Clusters are numbered by ascending mean volume:

| Cluster | Meaning | Mean volume |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Weekend night | 1,227 |
| 1 | Weekday night | 1,267 |
| 2 | Severe-weather hours (any time of day) | 2,998 |
| 3 | Weekend daytime | 3,914 |
| 4 | Weekday afternoon / evening | 4,848 |
| 5 | Weekday morning to midday | 5,174 |

**Association rules:** items are time of day (Night 00-05, Morning 06-11,
Afternoon 12-17, Evening 18-23), Weekday/Weekend and weather group (Clear,
Clouds, Rain/Drizzle, Low visibility, Severe weather); the consequent is the
Part 3 quartile `congestion_category`. Thresholds: support >= 0.01, confidence
>= 0.5, ranked by lift. Every combination of one to three items is counted
exactly (equivalent to Apriori for single-consequent rules). 44 rules pass; 14
are flagged `adds_information` (at least 2 percentage points more confident than
every simpler rule, `MIN_CONFIDENCE_GAIN`). Top informative rules: Night &
Weekend -> Low (90% confidence, lift 3.6); Night -> Low (85%, 3.4); Afternoon &
Weekend -> High (84%, 3.3); Afternoon & Weekday -> Severe (75%, 3.0). Time of
day and day type drive congestion; weather adds little.

Caveats: congestion levels derive from `traffic_volume`, which is also a K-means
feature, so clusters partly reflect congestion by construction. K-means depends
on scaling and k; rare weather types (Squall, Smoke, Fog) fall below the rule
support threshold. Cluster 2 groups by weather, not traffic level. Findings are
associations.

## Task 3: deep learning with explainability (LSTM + SHAP)

```powershell
python -m pip install shap tensorflow
python Part-3/lstm_model.py
```

An **LSTM** predicts the next hour's real `traffic_volume` from the previous 24
hourly readings (volume, temperature, rain, snow, clouds) plus the calendar of
the hour being predicted. Windows are built only from consecutive hourly rows
(the raw data has many gaps), giving 28,871 windows (19,067 train, 2,118
validation for early stopping, 7,686 test) with the same chronological 80/20
split as Task 1. The target is real volume, so the proxy-label caveat does not
apply. Only 7,686 of the 8,115 test hours have complete 24-hour histories;
performance immediately after gaps is not evaluated.

The script uses the original `Metro_Interstate_Traffic_Volume.csv` to restore raw
weather, checks it against the processed input, and fits monthly imputation and
standardization strictly before the first validation target. Supply both
`--input` and `--raw-input` when using a different matching dataset. The
target-hour holiday flag assumes a holiday calendar is available at prediction
time.

An LSTM is hard to explain directly, so **SHAP `TreeExplainer` is applied to a
Random Forest "twin"** trained on the same windows flattened into lag features
(`volume_lag_1` ... `volume_lag_24`, last-hour weather, hour, day of week,
weekend, holiday). The SHAP results describe the Random Forest, not the LSTM.
SHAP uses a random sample of 500 test windows. The forest receives only the
most recent weather values; the LSTM receives the entire weather history. Similar
model accuracy does not establish that the models use the same signals. SHAP
shares are model- and sample-specific, not causal evidence. In this dataset,
`snow_1h_lag_1` does not vary in the forest's training windows, and the forest
does not use the Snow weather category, so snow's effects cannot be inferred.

| Model | MAE | RMSE | R2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline: persistence (previous hour) | 585 | 812 | 0.830 |
| Baseline: same hour yesterday | 572 | 1,031 | 0.726 |
| Random Forest (lag features, SHAP twin) | 147 | 234 | 0.986 |
| Random Forest (calendar + weather only) | 271 | 448 | 0.948 |
| LSTM | 155 | 235 | 0.986 |

The evaluation is a one-hour-ahead forecast that needs fresh hourly readings.
The latest metrics and SHAP values are in the output CSVs and notebook.

It writes to `Part-3/outputs/`: `lstm_metrics.csv`, `lstm_test_predictions.csv`,
`lstm_run_info.json`, `lstm_model.keras`, `lstm_preprocessing.json`,
`lstm_forest.joblib`, `lstm_forest_nolag.joblib`, `shap_importance.csv`,
`shap_importance_no_lags.csv`, `shap_family_importance.csv` and the plots
(`lstm_training_curve.png`, `lstm_test_week.png`, `shap_summary_bar.png`,
`shap_beeswarm.png`, `shap_bar_no_lags.png`, `shap_local_*.png`). Logs go to
`Part-3/logs/lstm_model.log`; five MLflow runs (one per model and baseline) are
logged in the experiment `task3-deep-learning`. Options: the same `--input`,
`--output-dir`, `--log-file`, `--log-level` and MLflow options as Task 1, plus
`--raw-input`.
Training plus SHAP takes about 7 minutes on CPU. Notebook:
[`notebooks/Task3_deep_learning.ipynb`](notebooks/Task3_deep_learning.ipynb).
The notebook reads existing artifacts without overwriting them; run the script
first to refresh them.

For inference, load the Keras model and `lstm_preprocessing.json` together.
`predict_from_saved(model, sequence, calendar, metadata)` in `lstm_model.py`
standardizes inputs and inverse-scales and clips predictions to zero. `sequence`
must contain imputed, unscaled values in `sequence_features` order (temperature
in Celsius), shape `(n, 24, 5)`; `calendar` has shape `(n, 6)` in
`calendar_features` order. The JSON includes training-period monthly imputation
medians: temperature medians are in Kelvin, so convert to Celsius after
imputation. Load the joblib forest files only from trusted sources.

## Task 4: advanced technique (MLflow experiment audit and model registry)

```powershell
python Part-3/mlflow_registry.py
```

Run it after Tasks 1-3 (it needs `mlflow.db`, `mlartifacts/` and the same two CSVs as
Task 3). It logs to `Part-3/logs/mlflow_registry.log`, writes to `Part-3/outputs/`
(`mlflow_run_comparison.csv`, `mlflow_rerun_variability.csv`,
`mlflow_registry_summary.csv`, `mlflow_run_comparison.png`) and logs one run in the
experiment `task4-mlflow-registry`. Options: `--input`, `--raw-input`,
`--output-dir`, `--mlflow-uri`, `--mlflow-artifact-root`, `--experiment`,
`--log-file`, `--log-level`. Exit code 2 means a model failed the promotion gate.
Re-running does not create duplicate registered versions for the same source run.
Tests: `python -m unittest test_mlflow_registry` from `Part-3/`.

The read-only companion notebook
[`notebooks/Task4_mlflow_registry.ipynb`](notebooks/Task4_mlflow_registry.ipynb)
reviews the tracked runs, rerun variability and registered aliases. It does not
retrain models or change the registry; run the script above first to refresh its
saved outputs. To refresh the notebook output from the repository root:

```powershell
python -m jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace Part-3/notebooks/Task4_mlflow_registry.ipynb
```

**Why MLflow.** Tasks 1-3 produced 17 runs across three experiments, with
different models, metrics and artifacts. The deployment simulation (a later task) needs
to know *which* model to serve and to load it reproducibly. MLflow was already used
for tracking, so the Model Registry was the natural step towards MLOps, and it
needs no new infrastructure. The brief also recommends it for the MLOps component.

**How it was implemented.**
1. *Audit.* `collect_runs` reads every Task 1-3 run into one table and
   `rerun_variability` compares repeated runs of the same model.
2. *Selection.* The latest run per model is ranked: lowest test MAE for regression
   (Task 1 Random Forest; Task 3 forest and LSTM) and highest test PR AUC for the
   proxy classifier. Baselines and the calendar-only forest are not candidates.
3. *Registration.* Four models are registered: `traffic-volume-regressor`,
   `high-risk-classifier`, `next-hour-forecaster-rf` and `next-hour-forecaster-lstm`.
   Task 1's models were already MLflow models. The Task 3 forest is re-logged as a
   scikit-learn model with a signature. The Keras LSTM is wrapped in
   `LstmForecaster` (in `lstm_model.py`), a pyfunc model that bundles
   `lstm_preprocessing.json` and takes named `sequence` (n, 24, 5) and `calendar`
   (n, 6) inputs of raw, unscaled values. Each version is tagged with its source run,
   git commit, selection metric and rule.
4. *Promotion gate.* Each version is loaded back from the registry, scored on the
   rebuilt test set and compared with the metric logged by the training run
   (tolerance 0.05 vehicles/h for MAE, 1e-4 for PR AUC). Only a passing version gets
   an alias: `champion` for the best model of each problem, `challenger` for the
   other Task 3 model. Load one with
   `mlflow.sklearn.load_model("models:/traffic-volume-regressor@champion")` or
   `mlflow.pyfunc.load_model("models:/next-hour-forecaster-lstm@challenger")`.

| Registered model | Alias | Test metric (logged = registry) |
|---|---|---|
| `traffic-volume-regressor` (Random Forest, same hour) | champion | MAE 238.87 |
| `high-risk-classifier` (Random Forest, proxy label) | champion | PR AUC 0.9545 |
| `next-hour-forecaster-rf` (volume lags, needs last 24 h) | champion | MAE 147.32 |
| `next-hour-forecaster-lstm` (needs last 24 h) | challenger | MAE 155.16 |

Task 6 later adds versions 2 and 3 of `traffic-volume-regressor` and moves its
`champion` alias to v3 (see below). Re-running `mlflow_registry.py` sets that
alias back to v1, because it only knows the Task 1 run; re-run Task 6
`model_versions.py` afterwards to restore the Task 6 champion. The Task 4 scripts
ignore the `task6-*` experiments when auditing runs.

**Value added.**
- One comparison table and chart of every experiment, with git commit and metrics.
- It exposed a reproducibility problem: the two logged Task 3 LSTM runs differ by
  2.1% in MAE (155.2 vs 158.4; 60 vs 40 epochs), while the forests differ by
  under 0.01%. Single-run LSTM comparisons are therefore not stable. The two runs
  also use different data preparation (the weather imputation was changed in
  between), so the difference is not purely random-seed noise.
- Serving code can ask for `@champion` instead of hard-coding a run ID or file path.
  The gate shows the stored artifact, signature and preprocessing reproduce the
  reported score before an alias is granted; a replacement model is only promoted
  if it also passes.
- The LSTM can now be called without custom loading code, and bad input shapes are
  rejected by its signature.

**Limitations.**
- The tracking store is a local SQLite file with no authentication, so it is not a shared
  team server. `mlflow.db` is committed but `mlartifacts/` is git-ignored, so a fresh
  checkout has registry entries whose artifacts must be regenerated by re-running
  the Task 1-3 training scripts and this script.
- Data is not versioned in MLflow (only the git commit is recorded, and some runs were
  made with uncommitted code). The gate shows the artifact reproduces the logged
  number on the current data; it does not show the model is good.
- Selection and the gate use the test set; there is no separate selection set, so
  the champion scores are slightly optimistic. The Task 3 forest-vs-LSTM gap
  (8 vehicles/h MAE) is close to the LSTM's rerun spread (3 vehicles/h).
- Registering a model does not fix its problems: the classifier is trained on a
  proxy label that is partly circular (see Task 1), so its PR AUC is not
  accident-prediction skill.
- Aliases are moved by this script, not by an approval workflow; there is no
  automated retraining (monitoring is only simulated, in Task 6). Loading the registered models runs pickled
  code, so only load them from a trusted store. The LSTM model needs the same
  TensorFlow/Keras versions it was saved with.

## Task 5: travel-timing recommendation system

```powershell
python Part-3/recommendation_engine.py                 # writes tables, chart and backtest
python Part-3/recommendation_engine.py --day-type weekday --weather "rain/drizzle" --duration 2
```

The data is a single corridor, so the system recommends **when** to travel, not
which route. It uses only the labelled CSV (no MLflow needed) and logs to
`Part-3/logs/recommendation_engine.log`. Without `--day-type` it writes to
`Part-3/outputs/`: `recommendation_profile.csv` (expected volume per day type,

`recommendation_weather_check.csv`, `recommendation_profile.png` and
`recommendation_run_info.json`. With `--day-type` it prints one recommendation.
Options: `--weather`, `--duration`, `--earliest`, `--latest`, `--top-n`, `--input`,
`--output-dir`, `--log-file`, `--log-level`. Tests from `Part-3/`:
`python -m unittest test_recommendation_engine`. Notebook:
[`notebooks/Task5_recommendation_system.ipynb`](notebooks/Task5_recommendation_system.ipynb)
reads the script's saved chart and tables without overwriting them; run the
script first to refresh those outputs. It includes four worked recommendations
and an editable example for trying another journey. Open it and run all cells
in order, or refresh its saved cell outputs from the repository root with:

```powershell
python -m jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace Part-3/notebooks/Task5_recommendation_system.ipynb
```

**How it works.**
1. *Day type:* Weekday, Weekend or Holiday (the source's holiday/event flag is
   spread over the whole date; Holiday takes precedence). The source includes
   State Fair, so Holiday does not mean public holidays only. *Weather:* the Task 2
   groups (Clear, Clouds, Rain/Drizzle, Low visibility, Severe weather) or `Any`.
2. *Expected volume* is calculated from the historical hourly means for each day
   type and weather group. Weather-specific means are shrunk towards the same
   day type and hour's all-weather mean (`SHRINKAGE_HOURS` = 10, so a 10-hour
   cell is weighted 50/50). Ten is a heuristic, not a learned or validated
   threshold; shrinkage reduces the influence of sparse cells but does not
   guarantee reliability. `limited_evidence` flags windows with fewer than 10
   hours of the chosen weather per hour.
3. *Window search:* the lowest-mean window of the requested length that starts at or
   after 06:00 and ends by 19:00 (`DAYTIME`; change with `--earliest/--latest`),
   plus the next-best non-overlapping windows and the busiest window to avoid. A
   quieter evening window outside the requested range (up to 22:00) is added when
   it is at least 10% lower. The default excludes overnight, but a custom range
   can include it.
4. *Text:* a plain-language sentence with the window, volume, the corridor's
   four-level congestion scale (Part 3 quartiles), the saving versus the peak, a
   weather note and a not-a-forecast statement.

Example (`--day-type weekday`): "For a weekday journey, consider travelling between
10:00 AM and 11:00 AM, when historical traffic volumes are typically lower (about
4,453 vehicles per hour, 'High' on this corridor's four-level congestion scale).
This is the quietest option in the travel range, but volumes are still relatively
high. That is about 30% less than the busiest period, 4:00 PM to 5:00 PM ..."

**Backtest** (profile fitted on the first 80% of hours, checked on fully observed
days after 2017-10-26): the recommended 1-hour window carried 4,473 vehicles/h on
weekdays against 6,415 in the peak window and 5,288 for the average daytime hour,
and was below the daytime average on 228 of 229 days (99.6%). Weekend (97 days):
1,123 vs 4,452 peak; holiday (10 days): 2,476 vs 4,172 and below the daytime
average on 7 of 10. Adding weather to the hourly profile lowered test error only
slightly (MAE 306 to 303), largest in severe weather (505 to 489), which agrees
with the earlier finding that time of day and day type dominate.
The hourly comparison uses observed test-hour weather, not a weather forecast.
The final recommendations use the full history, including the backtest period.

**Limitations.**
- Descriptive averages from 2012-2018 for one corridor, not a live forecast; no
  incidents, roadworks, events or current weather feed. Weather is chosen by the
  user, not predicted.
- Volume is not safety: with no accident data (see the proxy statement above) a
  low-volume window is not shown to be safer, and low-visibility and severe-weather
  advice says so.
- The daytime range (06:00-19:00) is an assumption. A weekday's quietest daytime
  window is still in the "High" band, and the weekend answer (06:00-07:00) sits at
  the edge of the range.
- The 10-hour shrinkage and evidence cutoffs, 10% evening threshold and 5%
  weather-note threshold are chosen heuristics, not established travel or
  weather-effect thresholds. Weather comparisons are associations, not causal
  effects; shrinkage also pulls the comparison toward the all-weather average.
- Holiday cells combine very different holidays and have about 10 test days; the
  source also includes State Fair. Some Holiday x weather estimates have few
  observations and are flagged `limited_evidence`.
  A holiday that fell on a date without a 00:00 row is not detected.
- Windows are the lowest mean of hourly averages; day-to-day variation is not shown.
- The recommender uses the prepared traffic data and existing Task 2 weather groups,
  but does not load or predict with the trained or registered models. Its profile
  is an interpretable historical baseline, not an evaluation of those models.

| v1 | Random Forest (300 trees, `min_samples_leaf` 5; Task 1) | 238.87 | 400.09 | 0.9588 | 183.99 |
| v2 | Gradient Boosting (300 trees, depth 5, learning rate 0.1, subsample 0.8) | 239.79 | 399.72 | 0.9588 | 211.54 |
| v3 | Random Forest, tuned (`min_samples_leaf` 5, `max_features` 0.5) | **236.75** | 399.25 | 0.9589 | 193.72 |

Outputs: `outputs/model_versions.csv`, `model_versions.png`, `model_versions_rf_tuning.csv`.
The v2 settings are my choices, not tuned. v3 is chosen by a 4 x 4 grid over
`min_samples_leaf` (2, 5, 10, 20) and `max_features` (0.33, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0) with
3-fold time-ordered cross-validation on the **training period only**; it kept v1's
`min_samples_leaf` and differs from v1 only in `max_features`.

**Promotion rule (agreed):** every version is reloaded from the registry and scored on the
test period, and compared with the MAE its training run logged (the Task 4 gate,
tolerance 0.05 vehicles/h). `champion` moves only to a gate-passing version that beats
v1's test MAE; otherwise v1 stays champion. All three pass the gate and v3 beats v1, so
`champion` is now v3.

**Caveats:** v3 beats v1 by about 2 vehicles/h (under 1%) on a single split with no
repeated runs, which is not evidence of a real improvement. The champion choice uses the
test set, as in Task 4, so its score is slightly optimistic. `mlartifacts/` is git-ignored,
so v2 and v3 must be regenerated by `model_versions.py` on a fresh checkout.

### 6.3 Deployment mock-up (FastAPI)

`serve_api.py` loads `models:/traffic-volume-regressor@champion` at start-up. Endpoints:

| Endpoint | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `GET /health` | Liveness and whether the model loaded |
| `GET /model` | Served name, version, alias, weather groups accepted |
| `POST /predict` | Predicts same-hour volume from `timestamp`, `temp_c`, `rain_1h`, `snow_1h`, `clouds_all`, `weather_main`, `is_holiday` |
| `GET /monitor/status` | The latest `outputs/monitoring_status.json` (404 until `monitoring.py` has run) |

```powershell
curl.exe -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8000/predict -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{\"timestamp\":\"2018-06-14T17:00:00\",\"temp_c\":22.5,\"rain_1h\":0,\"snow_1h\":0,\"clouds_all\":40,\"weather_main\":\"Clouds\"}'
```

The reply has `predicted_traffic_volume` (vehicles/hour, floored at 0), the Part 2
`traffic_category` (Low < 4,500; Medium 4,500-5,500; High > 5,500, not the Part 3 quartile
categories), `is_congested` (> 5,500) and the model version. Inputs are validated
(clouds 0-100, rain/snow >= 0, temperature -60 to 60 C, known `weather_main` group);
bad input returns 422 and an unloaded model returns 503. The request is turned into the same
28 features as Task 1; a unit test checks that against `train_models.build_features`.
The timestamp's time zone is ignored (local time as in the dataset), and `is_holiday` is
supplied by the caller. It is a local mock-up: no authentication, TLS, rate limiting or
scaling, and the registered model is a pickle, so serve only models from a trusted store.

### 6.4 Monitoring and 6.5 alerting

`monitoring.py` replays the test period (2017-10-26 to 2018-09-30) as 12 consecutive
calendar months against the training period, using the champion model:

- **Feature distribution drift:** PSI for `temp_c`, `rain_1h`, `snow_1h`, `clouds_all`, `hour`
  and the `weather_main` mix (PSI over the group shares). Each month is compared with the
  **whole training period** and with the **same calendar month in the training period**
  (removing seasonality); the worse of the two drives the status. Bins come from training
  quantiles (10), right-closed, so zero-heavy columns split into zero / non-zero.
- **Prediction error drift:** the month's MAE against the model's full test-period MAE.

**Alert thresholds are heuristics.** They are common rules of thumb chosen for this
simulation (the brief gives no values), are not derived or validated on this data, and are
logged at the start of every run: a feature PSI >= 0.2 is an ALERT (0.1 to 0.2 is a warning
only) and a month MAE above 1.25 x the reference MAE is an ALERT (`PSI_WARNING`, `PSI_ALERT`,
`ERROR_RATIO_ALERT` in [`monitoring.py`](monitoring.py)). A month is **ALERT** if any check
alerts, else **PASS**. The **overall status is the latest month's status**. No month is
excluded for having few hours; the first month (26-31 Oct 2017) has only 126 hours and is
noisier.

Alerting: the status is written to `outputs/monitoring_status.json`, shown on the
colour-coded `outputs/monitoring_dashboard.html` (open it in a browser; banner
**PASS / Normal** or **ALERT / Requires investigation**, status by month, charts, both PSI
tables) and served by `GET /monitor/status`. Each alerting month and an overall ALERT are
logged at WARNING. The run is also logged to MLflow in `task6-monitoring`. Other outputs:
`monitoring_report.csv` (one row per month), `monitoring_feature_drift.csv`,
`monitoring_weather_shares.csv`.

**Result of the replay: overall ALERT / Requires investigation, with all 12 months
ALERT.** Prediction error exceeds the limit in 3 months (Dec 2017 1.47x, Jan 2018 1.32x,
Apr 2018 1.30x of the 236.75 reference). Feature drift alerts every month. The
whole-period reference alerts for `temp_c` in every month because temperature differs by
season. The same-month reference reduces this seasonal mismatch but still alerts in every month: for
`weather_main` in 9 months, `clouds_all` in 8, `rain_1h` in 7 and `temp_c` in 7; `hour` and
`snow_1h` never reach a threshold. These recorded distribution shifts do not establish a
cause: `rain_1h` is exactly 0 for all of 2017 in the source data (and non-zero in about 6%
of 2018 hours), which could reflect a recording gap; a month is one sample of about 700 hours.
Prediction error stayed within the limit in 9 of 12 months.

**Limitations.**
- A replay of historical data: there is no live feed, automated retraining, paging or
  approval workflow, and the model was not changed in response to an alert.
- The reference MAE is the model's own full test-period MAE, so months are compared with a
  period that contains them. In production the true volume would arrive later; here it is
  available immediately.
- Thresholds are conventions, and the "worse of two references" rule is deliberately
  sensitive, which is why every month alerts. Distribution change is not a diagnosis.
- PSI on a single month (about 700 hours, 126 for the first) is noisy; rare weather groups
  (Squall, Smoke, Fog) contribute noise to the `weather_main` PSI.
- Only the models and data from Tasks 1-4 are used; one corridor, uneven 2012-2018 coverage.

## Cautions

- **Leakage:** both labels are derived from `traffic_volume` and `weather_main`.
  `train_models.py` deliberately includes `weather_main` one-hot features, as
  the task requires weather encodings, and excludes the other columns below.
  Otherwise, do not use `traffic_volume`, `traffic_category`, `is_congested`,
  `congestion_category`, `weather_main*`, or `is_low_visibility` as features
  when predicting `high_risk`; predicting from them would be circular.
- **Not the Part 2 target:** `congestion_category` (quartiles, four classes) is
  separate from Part 2's `traffic_category`/`is_congested` (fixed 4,500/5,500
  thresholds). Both columns are kept and must not be mixed.
- **Quartiles use all rows.** For model evaluation, consider computing them on
  the training period only to avoid test-set information in the label.
- Use a time-based split (`timestamp`); coverage is uneven across years and
  2018 is partially covered.
- Rare weather classes (Squall 1 h, Smoke 15 h, Fog 201 h) give weak evidence.
